## 0. Most actionable for our Ally (short)

1. **The job-worker sleep/spin code changed several times after our fast build d7a7306** (Hookshot #2206, 2026-11-11 03:05 -05:00). All of these changes are in Longshot. Switches to A/B: `HOS_SHORT_SLEEP_POLLERS`, `HOS_SHORT_SLEEP_TIMER`, `HOS_SPIN_US`, `HOS_SPIN_OTHER_US`, `HOS_CORES`, `HOS_RESERVE_CORE`, `HOS_VCORE_ESCAPE_US` (section 1a). [H] for what the switches do; [L] for which change caused our regression.
2. **Readback waits are a known CPU-side stall on iGPUs**, with merged and WIP switches: `RX_VK_READBACK_LATE=newest` (merged), `RX_VK_READBACK_EARLY=1` (open PR), and `RX_VK_NO_READBACK_WAIT=1` (diagnostic, risk of exposure flashes). Measured gains on Steam Deck were +14 to +25% (section 1b). [M]
3. **Ally-specific stability recipes in Hookshot #997/#2891**: an 8 GB UMA buffer; `RX_VK_PER_QUEUE_FENCES=0` with `RX_VK_FORCE_MESH_FALLBACK=1`; `RX_VK_VRAM_MB=3072`; texture upload limits (section 1c). These switches date from Hookshot v2.0.x/2.1.0 and may be renamed or removed in Longshot. [M]
4. **First-run stutter options**: `RX_VK_PRECOMPILE_GRAPHICS=none` (Deck: 99 to 12 hitches, 7.5 to 3.8 GB memory), `RX_VK_QUICK_MS=8` (quick pipelines, merged, off by default), and the segment-heap fix (merged, AMD preparation -34%) (section 1d). [M]
5. **Anti-Lag 2 is done the "right" way in NX2navi**: pass `maxFPS` so the driver holds the frame cap and the app's own pacer stands down. FSR 3.1 FG uses AMD's swapchain with async off and a HUD-less copy (section 3). Its added code is "all rights reserved", so learn the ideas only and do not copy code into GPL Longshot. [H]

## 1. Longshot / Hookshot issues and PRs

### 1a. Job threads, sleeps, spins, virtual cores (our ~20% regression)

**How it works now** ([docs/cpu-profile.md](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Longshot/src/branch/main/docs/cpu-profile.md), Ryzen 7 5700X3D 8C/16T + RX 9070 XT) [H]
- Idle game job workers (`ModuleSystemWorker*`, `OptimizeChangeBanc`) sleep for 20 µs and re-check their queues, about 7,600 `SleepThread` calls a second in Hyrule Field.
- A host sleep under 1 ms loops on `SwitchToThread` instead of using the OS timer, which would wake about 0.5 ms late. Each sleeping worker therefore keeps a processor busy.
- `spin_until_set` spins up to `HOS_SPIN_US` (20 µs) before an event or queue waiter sleeps. That costs 13 to 17% of a core per scene.
- `vc_acquire` makes a guest thread wait for one of the game's 6 virtual cores. It costs about 6 to 7% of a core, and a thread escapes after `HOS_VCORE_ESCAPE_US` (2 s).
- The frame is paced by hand-offs between threads, not by the GPU. A later review saw the main thread waiting on hand-offs about 50% of the time (~7.6 ms per frame; [#156](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Longshot/pulls/156), dukenheimer, 2026-11-13).

**#2709 in detail** (2026-11-12) [H]
- On Windows with 7+ logical processors, at most `HOS_SHORT_SLEEP_POLLERS` game threads yield-poll at once. The default is processors/4, which is **4 on the Ally**. The rest wait on the high-resolution timer.
- Non-serial threads spin `HOS_SPIN_OTHER_US` (5 µs). Serial threads (names containing "MainThread", or "Presentation Thread") keep `HOS_SPIN_US` (20 µs).
- Results, 5700X3D at 16 LP: CPU per frame -4.4%, fps +1.3%. Masked to 8 LP: CPU per frame -25%.
- Not tested on Steam Deck or real 6/8-thread CPUs. Measured on a pre-#2602 head. [M]

**Switches** (values from the PRs and docs above) [H]
- `HOS_SHORT_SLEEP_POLLERS`: `0` removes the cap. Default is processors/4 when there are more than 6, otherwise 0.
- `HOS_SHORT_SLEEP_TIMER`:
  - unset: the processor-count rule
  - on: the timer for **all** short sleeps, including the host's frame limiter and audio. The cap is ignored.
  - off: yield for all short sleeps.
  - An earlier test of "timer for all" at 16 LP saw 250 ms frames in 3 of 32 runs, against 0 of 38 without it.
- `HOS_SPIN_US` (20; `0` disables spinning; `200` was slower per design.md) and `HOS_SPIN_OTHER_US` (5 above 6 LP; `20` restores one spin for all threads).
- `HOS_VCORES=0` disables virtual cores.
- `HOS_CORES=1..8` sets the cores the game sees (6 by default).
- `HOS_RESERVE_CORE=0` stops reserving a core for the replay. A core is reserved by default with 8+ logical processors, which includes the Ally.
- `HOS_VCORE_ESCAPE_US` (2 s) and `HOS_VCORE_SLICE_US` (2 ms).
- `HOS_PIN_RENDER=0` gave +9 to +24% on hybrid Intel and was neutral on Zen 3 (Hookshot [#995](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Hookshot/issues/995)).
- Profiling: `HOS_PROFILE=5`, `HOS_SAMPLE_ALL`, `HOS_HLE_STATS=1`, `HOS_PRESENT_TRACE`, `HOS_TLS_TRACE=1`, `HOS_CHAIN`. Note that `HOS_SAMPLE_ALL` can crash the game (Longshot [#383](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Longshot/issues/383), 2026-11-14, open).
- Switches go in `longshot_env.txt` beside the exe. Some local-only switches are refused from that file (`HOS_INPUT_SCRIPT`, `HOS_AUDIO_DUMP`, `RX_VK_FRAME_LOG`; [PR #62](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Longshot/pulls/62)). Set those as real environment variables instead. [M]

**Suggested A/B on the Ally** [L]
- Approximate pre-#2709 behaviour with `HOS_SHORT_SLEEP_POLLERS=0` and `HOS_SPIN_OTHER_US=20`.
- Then try in the other direction: `HOS_SHORT_SLEEP_POLLERS=1` or `2`, and `HOS_SPIN_US=5`.
- Read the log's `vcores:` lines (takes, waits, kicks, yields, escapes) and "ran without a core" lines to see whether #2302's extra vcore traffic is what grew.
- On a shared-power APU, spinning and yield-polling threads also take package power from the 780M. That makes polling cost more on the Ally than on a desktop. [L]

### 1b. Readback waits (CPU stalls that hit iGPUs hardest)
- [#241](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Longshot/issues/241) (issue, 2026-11-13, RTX 3050 Ti 4 GB laptop) [M]:
  - Job threads waited about 6.5 ms on readbacks for the whole submission.
  - rc.3 ran at 38 fps; `RX_VK_NO_READBACK_WAIT=1` gave 47.5 fps, the same as Hookshot v2.1.0.
  - The reporter's reading: the by-part wait is skipped when not every pool fits in the VRAM mirrors.
  - `HOS_NO_PACING=1` from old guides makes movies and menus run fast on Longshot.
- [PR #238](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Longshot/pulls/238) (merged 2026-11-14 01:02) [M]:
  - `RX_VK_READBACK_LATE=newest` (or `newest,3980`) reads the named ranges without waiting, so they hold last frame's values.
  - Steam Deck OLED at 1600 MHz: 24.3 to 30.3 fps (heavy view), 21.9 to 26.5 (Hyrule Field), 23.4 to 26.8 (Kokiri).
  - Risk: the exposure meter can read stale values. No flashes were found in the tested scenes.
  - `RX_VK_LOG_READBACK_RANGES=1` helps find which ranges wait.
- [PR #303](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Longshot/pulls/303) (open, labels gpu/igpu and platform/steam-deck, 2026-11-13/14) [M]:
  - `RX_VK_READBACK_EARLY=1` makes a read wait only for its writer's part.
  - Steam Deck: 11.1 to 12.7 fps (+14%); readback waits dropped from ~23 ms to ~1.4 ms.
  - `RX_VK_READBACK_EARLY_GAP` defaults to 16. Diagnostics: `RX_VK_LOG_READBACK_WAITS=1` and `RX_VK_LOG_RB_WRITERS=1`.
  - Off when `RX_VK_PARALLEL` is 2 or more. Review still had requested changes on 11-14.
- Not known: whether the Ally on Windows gets VRAM mirrors (the device-local heap is the UMA carve-out) or falls into the whole-submission wait. **Check with `RX_VK_LOG_READBACK_WAITS=1`.** [L]
- Older related switches from Hookshot [#995](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Hookshot/issues/995) (v2.0.x): `RX_VK_CAPTURE_WAIT=0` (removes the late-page wait, causes flashes), `RX_VK_MIRROR_MB` (do not set low), `RX_VK_PREFETCH_CHUNKS=2` (1% lows 30.9 to 35.9 on an RX 9070 XT). [M]

## 2. Why NVIDIA reportedly runs it better than AMD
- **The shaders are NVIDIA-native.** The Switch 2 GPU's code is translated by Eden's recompiler, and AMD needs workarounds that NVIDIA does not. [L] as a framing; the specifics below are [H] from the named PRs.
- **Warp 32 vs wave64** (Hookshot [#1507](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Hookshot/pulls/1507), 2026-11-10):
  - The translator assumed 32-lane warps. On RDNA 3 (RX 7800 XT reports `0xf0`), only the vertex stage cannot be pinned to wave32, so it takes the wider path.
  - AMD's proprietary driver gets fragment-stage subgroup shuffles wrong. The fallback turns each shuffle into 32 broadcasts.
  - Bisect with `RX_VK_WARP64=0|1` and `RX_VK_FRAG_SHUFFLE_FALLBACK=0|1`.
- **Semantics** (Hookshot [#138](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Hookshot/pulls/138), RX 9070 XT, 2026-11-07):
  - AMD returns the NaN operand from min/max; NVIDIA returns the other one. Fixed with NaN-safe ops, including the atomic min/max loops.
  - AMD writes garbage to colour components the shader does not write, so write masks were narrowed.
  - AMD does not compare depth through non-comparing samplers.
  - AMD bounds-checks storage-buffer reads past their size; NVIDIA reads through.
  - `RX_VK_SUBMIT_DELAY_US=1000` "helped considerably" with focus-dependent pacing.
- **Bounds and alignment**: Hookshot [#2442](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Hookshot/pulls/2442) (merged 2026-11-11) puts storage buffers on 16-byte boundaries and bounds-checks texture decode stores on AMD.
- **Compressed textures**: [#2001](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Hookshot/pulls/2001) (open) and [#893](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Hookshot/pulls/893) handle BC texture views, attachments and storage on AMD. `RX_VK_ASTC_RGBA8=1` / `RX_VK_ASTC_BC7=1` work around blocky textures. [H]
- **Mesh and task shaders**: AMD's Windows driver lost the device on Navi 21/22, and from 2026-11-14 also Navi 23 ([PR #219](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Longshot/pulls/219)), so those use the compute fallback (`RX_VK_TASK_MESH_FALLBACK`, `RX_VK_MESH_SHADERS=compute|native`). [H]
  - RDNA 3 keeps native mesh shaders by default. The 780M is not mentioned.
  - RDNA 4 hit a task-stage hang (Hookshot [#996](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Hookshot/issues/996)). Hookshot [#1747](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Hookshot/pulls/1747) worked around AMD task shader timeouts.
  - The fallback costs VRAM and is "usually a little slower".
- **Pipeline compile cost**: the AMD optimizer took 5-13 s per pipeline (PR #234) and contended on the NT heap lock (PR #15). After compile faults, AMD builds run one at a time (`RX_VK_DRIVER_FAULTS=2`). NVIDIA's cost is described as milliseconds, but that is an estimate. [M]
- **The game's own upscaler**: its temporal network uses matrix ops. On an RX 7800 XT these ran in software, so a plain stand-in was used. `RX_VK_TAAU_KEYS=-` forces the real network for about +1.3 ms GPU (Hookshot #995). [M]
- **Submission cost on RADV/Linux**: about 13 ms per `vkQueueSubmit2`, suspected userptr revalidation of imported host memory. Batching is on by default on RADV only (`RX_VK_BATCH`); forcing it on an RTX 4060 Ti dropped 25 to 16 fps (Hookshot [#413](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Hookshot/issues/413), 2026-11-08). Windows AMD was not tested. [M]
- **Defaults tuned on NVIDIA**:
  - Game-ordered barriers are on for NVIDIA only (`RX_VK_GAME_ORDER`, in WIP #288; about a tenth off GPU frame time on an RTX 5090).
  - "Robust buffers Off" can be faster on some NVIDIA cards.
  - The benchmark tracker [#69](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Longshot/issues/69) has only RTX 4070 data.
  - Many PRs were measured only on NVIDIA. [H]
- **Not found**: an AMD-specific float-atomics issue (the float-add fix [PR #191](https://git.magicbean.dev/MagicBeanDev/Longshot/pulls/191) is Intel), an LDS barrier issue, and AMD present-mode problems.

## 3. NX2navi (github.com/tangerineturtle1/NX2navi)
- **What it is**: a separate static recompilation of OoT 1.1.1 (Switch 2) to x86-64 Windows with a Vulkan renderer. 4.0k stars, 304 forks, 1,068 commits, read 2026-11-14.
  - License: MIT base, but additions are "all rights reserved" (LICENSE-ADDITIONS). **Do not copy its code into GPL Longshot; reuse ideas only.** [H]
  - Known issue: integrated AMD GPUs can fail at the title screen because the host memory heap is too small.
  - Requires `VK_EXT_external_memory_host` and `VK_EXT_mesh_shader`. 16 GB RAM machines can run out of memory in Hyrule Field.
- **FSR 3.1 upscaling** ([runtime/vk_ffx.cpp](https://github.com/tangerineturtle1/NX2navi/blob/main/runtime/vk_ffx.cpp)) [H]:
  - Loads `amd_fidelityfx_vk.dll` at run time (`LoadLibraryA`; `ffxCreateContext`, `ffxConfigure`, `ffxQuery`, `ffxDispatch`). API headers are in `runtime/third_party/ffx_api/`.
  - `ffxCreateContextDescUpscale` is chained to `ffxCreateBackendVKDesc`, with flags HDR and AUTO_EXPOSURE, and DYNAMIC_RESOLUTION when enabled.
  - Inputs: the linear HDR scene, D32F depth, and the game's camera motion vectors (scale 1.0). `preExposure` is 1.0. There are no reactive or transparency masks.
  - Keeps up to 4 contexts by size, trimmed after 90 s unused, and idles the device before destroying one.
  - **Routes FFX's own pipeline creation through the runtime's pipeline cache** (`vk_ffx_proc_addr` replaces `vkCreate*Pipelines`; `NX2NAVI_FFX_PIPE_CACHE`).
- **FSR 3.1 frame generation** ([runtime/vk_fg.cpp](https://github.com/tangerineturtle1/NX2navi/blob/main/runtime/vk_fg.cpp)) [H]:
  - Uses AMD's frame-generation swapchain (`FFX_API_CREATE_CONTEXT_DESC_TYPE_FGSWAPCHAIN_VK`) in place of the window swapchain. All swapchain calls go through the function table AMD returns.
  - The HUD-less copy is taken before the HUD draws. Depth and motion vectors are shared with the upscaler.
  - `allowAsyncWorkloads=false`, with no async compute queue. Pacing is left to AMD's swapchain threads.
  - Generation pauses while the menu is open and resets on camera cuts. Frames larger than `maxRenderSize` are skipped.
  - `vk_fg4.cpp` handles the FSR 4 FG path.
- **Anti-Lag 2** ([runtime/vk_lowlat.c](https://github.com/tangerineturtle1/NX2navi/blob/main/runtime/vk_lowlat.c)) [H]:
  - INPUT stage at frame start (the first pad read or image acquire after a present). The guest thread gives up its virtual core around that call, because the driver waits inside it.
  - PRESENT stage before the host present, with the same `frameIndex`.
  - **`maxFPS` carries the frame cap, so the driver holds the limit and the app's own pacer does nothing.** If more than half of 60 frames come early, the driver is judged not to hold the limit, and the app limiter takes over with `maxFPS=0`.
  - The driver's sleep is skipped if no present has gone out for 200 ms. The extension is enabled only when the setting is on at start, so changing it needs a restart.
- **Threading**: 3 virtual cores with priority-based and asynchronous preemption (`runtime/kernel.c`), a recorder thread fed from a ring (`vk_rec.c`), and `par_jobs.c`. [H]
- **What we could learn for the 780M** [L]:
  1. Let AL2's `maxFPS` do the frame cap, and give up the virtual core while the driver sleeps.
  2. Cache FSR pipelines through our cache, so they are not recompiled at each start.
  3. Start FG with async off. GPUOpen gives the same advice.
  4. Compare the virtual-core model: 3 cores with preemption vs Hookshot's 6 cores with 2 s escapes.

## 6. RDNA 3 iGPU / Vulkan tips for a renderer like this
Sources: the [GPUOpen RDNA performance guide](https://gpuopen.com/learn/rdna-performance-guide/), the [AL2 page](https://gpuopen.com/anti-lag-2/) and the [FSR 3.1 docs](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fidelityfx_sdk/techniques/super-resolution-interpolation/). Ally-specific points are marked [L].
- **Pipelines**: share one pipeline cache across runs, and compile on worker threads (the driver does not spawn its own). Avoid just-in-time builds, and leave dynamic states off when they never change. On Windows AMD, the segment heap matters (PR #15). [H]
- **Submits**: minimise them, and submit only at queue syncs or the end of the frame. Use at least 10 draws per command buffer, one pool per thread per frame in flight, and ONE_TIME_SUBMIT. Avoid secondary command buffers. [H]
- **Barriers**: batch them, skip read-to-read barriers, avoid GENERAL layouts, and use only the stage and access bits needed. [H]
- **Descriptors**: keep the pipeline layout under about 13 DWORDs. Use immutable samplers, push constants for per-draw data only, and bindless where possible. With robust access, a dynamic buffer costs 4 DWORDs instead of 2. [H]
- **Render targets**: avoid STORAGE usage and MUTABLE formats on render targets, and write all channels so compression stays on. Use D32 depth, reversed. [H]
- **Memory on an APU**: budget 80% of what `VK_EXT_memory_budget` reports and sub-allocate 256 MB blocks. Use DEVICE_LOCAL|HOST_VISIBLE for CPU-written constants and never read it back. On Windows the "VRAM" heap is the Armoury Crate UMA carve-out, so test 4, 6 and 8 GB: larger is more stable per #997, but leaves less system RAM per #388. [H]/[L]
- **Waves**: use workgroups that are a multiple of 64, and 64-thread workgroups for async compute. Async compute does poorly beside export-bound shaders. [H]
- **Present modes**: query the supported modes first. AMD's guide prefers FIFO_RELAXED for V-Sync on and IMMEDIATE for off. [H]
  - The Ally's 120 Hz VRR panel will not stay in range at ~33 fps, so a 40 fps cap (an even divisor of 120 Hz) is a pacing option; a #997 user suggested 40. [L]
- **Anti-Lag 2**: needs RDNA 1 or newer, Windows 10/11, and Adrenalin 24.9.1+ for Vulkan. Enable the `antiLag` feature at device creation. [H]
  - Call INPUT just before input is sampled and PRESENT before present, both with the same frameIndex.
  - Validate with AMD's latency monitor: about 1-2 frames when working; above 3 means an integration problem.
  - One report says the Vulkan extension is not the same implementation as the driver's DirectX Anti-Lag 2 stack. [M]
- **FSR 3.1 frame generation**: get the present-queue integration right with async off, then try async. Call prepare at upscale time, and increment the frame ID by exactly 1. [H]
  - Aim for at least ~60 fps before FG, and do not combine it with AFMF or overlays. Swapchain replacement is not allowed in exclusive fullscreen.
  - At our ~33 fps base, FG will add noticeable latency, which AL2 only partly offsets. [L]
- **CPU and power on a shared-TDP APU**: CPU spin and yield-polling uses package power the 780M could use. Cutting polling (1a) is worth more on the Ally than desktop numbers suggest. [L]
- **Readback and sync**: split submissions with timeline values per part, so a CPU read waits only for its writer (Hookshot #2602, Longshot #303). This matters most where there is no VRAM mirror. [H]/[M]
